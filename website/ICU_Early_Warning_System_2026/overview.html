Overview

The problem, the data, and the end-to-end approach behind the ICU Early Warning System.

Problem statement

Patients admitted to an Intensive Care Unit can deteriorate rapidly, and early physiological changes (rising heart rate, falling blood pressure, rising lactate) often precede a clinically obvious crisis by several hours. The goal of this project is to build a model that flags which patients are at risk of deterioration using only the vitals and labs that are already routinely captured in the first hours of an ICU stay, so clinicians can prioritize their attention.

Problem typeBinary classification (at risk / not at risk of deterioration)
Target variableDeterioration / mortality outcome flag
Available dataAge, gender, heart rate (mean/min/max), systolic & diastolic BP (mean/min/max), admission metadata, optional labs (lactate, creatinine)
Missing dataLab values (lactate, creatinine) are frequently absent — not every patient is drawn early
Main riskMissing a true deterioration (false negative) is clinically more costly than a false alarm
Success metricROC-AUC, sensitivity, and macro-F1, tuned for an imbalanced outcome

Dataset

The model is trained on COMPLETE_ICU_RISK_DATASET.csv, a MIMIC-derived ICU cohort with vitals, admission context, and deterioration outcomes. See Data & EDA for the full exploratory analysis, or download the raw file from Downloads.
