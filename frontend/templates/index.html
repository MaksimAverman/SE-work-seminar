How to use: Enter the patient's demographic data and vital signs recorded during the first 12 hours of ICU admission. The model will compute derived indicators (Shock Index, MAP, etc.) automatically and estimate the deterioration risk.
👤

Patient Information

Basic demographic data from the patient's medical record.
Patient's age at ICU admission. Model trained on ages 3–90.
Hour the patient was admitted to the ICU (24h format, 0–23). Night admissions (22:00–06:00) may carry higher risk.
Weekday of ICU admission. Weekend staffing may affect outcomes.
❤️

Heart Rate (bpm)

Heart rate readings from the cardiac monitor during the first 12 hours. Enter the average, lowest, and highest values recorded.
Average HR over the monitoring period. Normal resting: 60–100 bpm. Above 100 = tachycardia.
The lowest HR recorded. Below 50 = bradycardia warning.
The highest HR recorded. Large difference between min and max = hemodynamic instability.
🩸

Blood Pressure (mmHg)

Blood pressure readings from the first 12 hours. Systolic = upper number (pressure during heartbeat). Diastolic = lower number (pressure between beats). Enter average, lowest, and highest values from the monitor.

Systolic (upper)

Normal: 90–140 mmHg
Below 90 = severe hypotension ⚠️
Above 180 = hypertensive crisis

Diastolic (lower)

Normal: 60–90 mmHg
Below 50 = impaired perfusion
Above 120 = critical
🧪

Lab Results (optional)

Worst (maximum) lab values from the first 12 hours. Optional — leave blank if unavailable and the cohort median will be used. These markers strongly improve risk estimation.
Highest lactate recorded. Normal: < 2.0. Above 4.0 = tissue hypoperfusion / possible sepsis 🔴
Highest creatinine recorded. Normal: 0.6–1.3. Above 2.0 = renal dysfunction ⚠️
